# Mock Learner Gremlin: Module 05 (Homework for Utah)

**Date:** 2026-09-16
**Two personas:** Denise (diligent), Marcus (scripted-flaw). Both given only `MODULE-README.md` and a fresh copy of `workshop-folder/`. Verification performed directly by the orchestrator.

**Process note:** this module requires genuine live web research, not local fixtures. Both nested `claude -p` calls used `--allowedTools "WebSearch,WebFetch,Write,Edit,Read,Bash"` alongside `--permission-mode acceptEdits`, and both personas did real, live research against the real internet - nothing here was fabricated or simulated.

**Operational note:** the scripted-flaw persona's Attempt Agent exited before its own nested `claude -p` research call had actually finished running in the background - a real instance of the exact silent-completion risk this Gremlin's own documentation already warns about for workflow-parallelized runs (Design Principle 8), here showing up even in manual orchestration once a long-running live-research call outlasted its own calling agent's turn. Caught by checking the scratch directory and the live process table directly rather than trusting the agent's own "done" status, waiting for the real process to finish, then completing the write-up from the actual resulting file.

## Denise (diligent) — PASS 5/5, genuinely strong result

Real live research against Eventbrite, Ticket Tailor, and Prekindle. The nested session self-reported, unprompted, that Ticket Tailor's real pricing page returned a 403 and its numbers came from search-indexed snippets rather than a direct read - an honest disclosure of its own research limitation, not papered over. Denise's own independent fact-check (Part 4) hit the identical 403 on the same URL, an unplanned real confirmation that the caveat was genuine rather than a lazy excuse, and separately verified the Eventbrite numbers matched exactly. Her `answers.txt` correctly flags the resulting asymmetry: the platform actually *recommended* is the one least independently verified. Checker: `PASS (5/5)`, independently reconfirmed.

## Marcus (scripted-flaw) — FAIL 4/5, gate discriminated correctly

**Scripted flaw:** a literal misunderstanding baked into his own request - Marcus believed the assignment meant comparing one well-known platform's three pricing tiers, not three different platforms, and asked Claude Code to research Eventbrite's Essentials/Professional/Premium tiers accordingly.

**What happened:** genuinely excellent, honest research that happened to answer the wrong assignment. The nested session discovered and reported, unprompted, that Eventbrite's real current U.S. storefront doesn't even offer the three-tier split anymore - it collapsed to one "Flex" plan - and resolved this by citing Eventbrite's Singapore storefront (which still shows the tier split) alongside the real U.S. pricing page, naming the discrepancy explicitly in the brief itself. This is real research quality, not a lazy attempt - it just answers a different question than Johnny actually needed.

**Checker result:** `FAIL: comparison-brief.md cites source URLs from at least 3 distinct sites (found 2)` - correctly, since every cited URL was some form of `eventbrite.sg` or `eventbrite.com`, two registrable domains, not three. `RESULT: FAIL (4/5)`, independently reconfirmed. The gate caught exactly the flaw baked into the request, on real content, without needing to know anything about the assignment's intent beyond counting distinct source domains.

**Worth noting, not a defect:** the checker treated `eventbrite.sg` and `eventbrite.com` as two distinct sites (not collapsing them as "the same company"), which happens to be defensible under the module's own stated rule ("different websites... not three different pages on the same company's site" - different top-level domains arguably are different sites even under one brand) but is worth flagging as a real edge case for anyone tightening this check later, in the same family as Module 10's `.co.uk`-style multi-part-TLD finding.

## What this run establishes

Both personas did genuine, live, honest research - no fabricated citations, no simulated web pages. The gate correctly passed a strong diligent attempt (including one that self-identified its own weakest, unverified claim) and correctly failed a genuine, well-researched-but-wrong-assignment attempt on the exact dimension the module's checker is designed to catch (source diversity), without needing any judgment about assignment intent. This module's "no answer key, structural checks only" design (explicitly named as the honest limit in its own README) held up: the checker never needed to know whether any specific price was *true*, only whether the brief was actually built from three distinct sources - which is exactly the claim it makes about itself.

## Honest limit

Same as every module so far: neither persona used a live interactive session; this is real, live, un-simulated web research, but conducted non-interactively. Per the module's own honest limit, the checker (and this Gremlin) cannot and does not verify whether any cited page's content is accurate at the moment of reading - only that real, distinct, cited sources exist.
